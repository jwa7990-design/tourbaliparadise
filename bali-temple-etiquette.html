Bali temple etiquette

Bali has thousands of temples, from family shrines to great sea temples. Visitors are welcome at many of them. Here’s how to visit respectfully.

Visit temples with Aris

What to wear

  • A sarong (kamen) tied around the waist, covering your legs. Men and women both wear one.
  • A sash (selendang) tied around the waist over the sarong.
  • Covered shoulders. Bring a light top or scarf.
  • Most temples that welcome visitors lend or rent sarongs and sashes at the entrance, often for a small donation.

Inside the temple

  • Some inner courtyards are for worshippers only. Look for signs or ask before going in.
  • Don’t climb on shrines or walls, and don’t sit or stand higher than a priest or the offerings.
  • Walk around, not in front of, people who are praying.
  • Keep your voice down and avoid public displays of affection.
  • Many temples ask that women who are menstruating do not enter. This is usually posted at the entrance.

Offerings

You’ll see small woven trays of flowers, rice and incense everywhere: on doorsteps, on pavements and in shrines. These canang sari are daily offerings. Try not to step on them. If you do by accident, don’t worry, but don’t pick them up or move them.

Photos and ceremonies

Photos are usually fine in the outer courtyards, but ask before photographing people praying or taking part in a ceremony, and never use flash during prayers. Drones need permission.

If you come across a ceremony or procession, stand to the side and let it pass. Ceremonies follow the Balinese calendar, so some temples close to visitors on certain days.

Visit with someone who can explain

Temples are far more interesting when someone explains what you’re seeing: the shrines, the colours, the offerings and the ceremonies. That’s a big part of Experience Bali as a Local, where sarongs and temple donations are included.

Questions

What do I wear to a temple in Bali?

A sarong and sash around the waist and covered shoulders. Most visitor temples lend or rent sarongs at the entrance.

Can tourists go inside Balinese temples?

Many temples welcome visitors to the outer courtyards. Some inner areas are for worshippers only.

Can I take photos at Balinese temples?

Usually yes in the outer areas. Ask before photographing people praying, and don’t use flash during ceremonies.
