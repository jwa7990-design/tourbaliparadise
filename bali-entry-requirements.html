Bali entry requirements and official links

Visas, arrival cards and the tourist levy confuse a lot of visitors, and copycat websites charge extra for things that are cheap or free. Here’s what most travellers need, and the official places to get it.

*The visa fee is the amount listed by the UK government on 19 November 2026. An increase has been announced, so check the current fee on the official site when you apply.

What most visitors need

  • A passport valid for at least 6 months after the day you arrive, with at least 2 blank pages.
  • A visa, unless your nationality is exempt. Most tourists use the visa on arrival: 30 days, single entry, extendable once for another 30 days. You can buy it online before you fly (the e-VOA) or at the airport counter.
  • Proof of your onward or return journey.
  • The All Indonesia arrival card, completed online or in the app within 3 days (72 hours) before you arrive. It replaces the old customs and health forms and is free.
  • The Bali tourist levy of Rp 150,000 per person, paid online or on arrival.
  • A yellow fever certificate, only if you’re arriving from a country listed as a yellow fever risk.

Step by step

  • A week or more before you fly: check whether you need a visa on the immigration website, then apply for the e-VOA there. Make sure it’s approved before you travel.
  • Within 3 days of arriving: fill in the All Indonesia arrival card and keep the QR code it gives you.
  • Before you land or on arrival: pay the Bali tourist levy on the Love Bali website or app.
  • When you land: go through immigration and customs with your passport and QR codes ready. Connect to the airport Wi-Fi and message Aris if he’s collecting you.

How to avoid being ripped off

  • Only use websites ending in .go.id for the visa, arrival card and levy. Search ads often show lookalike sites first.
  • The arrival card is free. Any site or email asking you to pay for it is not official.
  • Be wary of emails asking for payment after you apply. Check the web address before you click or pay.
  • You don’t need an agent for a standard tourist visa. Agents can be useful for complicated visas, but they charge for it.
  • At the airport, ignore anyone offering to “speed up” immigration for cash, and pre-arrange your transfer so you’re not negotiating taxi fares after a long flight.

Staying longer

The 30-day visa on arrival can be extended once, for another 30 days. Do it before the first 30 days run out: overstaying costs Rp 1,000,000 per day. If you applied online, you can extend online through the immigration website. For longer stays or other purposes, check the visa options on the official immigration website.

Check your own government’s advice

Rules can change at short notice, and they differ by nationality. Your government’s travel advice is kept up to date:

  • Australia: Smartraveller: Indonesia
  • United Kingdom: GOV.UK: Indonesia entry requirements
  • New Zealand: SafeTravel: Indonesia
  • United States: travel.state.gov: Indonesia
  • Canada: travel.gc.ca: Indonesia

This page was last checked on 19 November 2026. It’s a guide, not legal advice: always confirm on the official websites before you travel.

Questions

Do I need a visa for Bali?

Most visitors do. Many nationalities can get a 30-day visa on arrival, online before travel (e-VOA) or at the airport. Some nationalities are exempt. Check on the official immigration website, evisa.imigrasi.go.id.

How much is the Bali visa on arrival?

The UK government lists it as Rp 500,000 for 30 days (checked 19 November 2026). An increase has been announced, so confirm the current fee on evisa.imigrasi.go.id.

Is the All Indonesia arrival card free?

Yes. It is free on allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id and in the official app. Don’t pay any website that charges for it.

How much is the Bali tourist levy?

Rp 150,000 per person, paid on lovebali.baliprov.go.id, in the Love Bali app, or on arrival.

How do I spot a fake visa website?

Official Indonesian government sites end in .go.id. Anything else is not the government.
