Solo travel in Bali

Bali is one of the easiest places in Asia to travel alone. A few practical tips make it easier still.

Plan a solo day

Getting around

Most visitors use ride-hailing apps such as Grab and Gojek for short trips, and a private driver for longer days out. Traffic in South Bali is heavy, so distances on the map take longer than they look.

Thinking of renting a scooter? You need a valid licence that covers motorbikes, usually with an International Driving Permit, and police checks on tourists are common. Always wear a helmet. If you’re not an experienced rider, riding as a passenger with a local is a safer way to explore.

Staying safe

  • Use the same common sense as anywhere: keep valuables out of sight and your bag on the side away from the road.
  • Share your plans or live location with someone at home when you head out for the day.
  • Drink bottled or filtered water, not tap water.
  • In an emergency, call 112.

Money and SIM cards

Indonesian rupiah (IDR) is the currency. Cards are accepted in many cafés and hotels, but small warungs and markets often take cash only. Use ATMs attached to banks where you can.

Tourist SIM cards and eSIMs are easy to get on arrival, at the airport or in phone shops. You’ll need your passport to register a local SIM.

Meeting people

Hostels, yoga studios, cooking classes and group activities are easy ways to meet other travellers. Many solo visitors also like having one day with a local who can explain what they’re seeing and answer every question.

A day with a local host

On the Solo Traveller Companion, you spend a half or full day with Aris, planned around you: a first-day orientation, countryside and rice fields, a food morning, or a full day exploring. Pickup and drop-off are at reception or the entrance, and your route is agreed before the day.

Questions

Is Bali safe for solo travellers?

Bali is generally considered a welcoming place to travel alone. Take the usual precautions: watch your belongings, be careful on the roads, and let someone know your plans.

Is it safe to ride a scooter in Bali?

Roads are busy and accidents are common among visitors. Only ride if you’re experienced, licensed and wearing a helmet. Otherwise ride as a passenger or use a car.

What is the emergency number in Bali?

112 is Indonesia’s general emergency number.
