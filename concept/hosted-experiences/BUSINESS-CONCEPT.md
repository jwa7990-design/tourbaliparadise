## The answer first

**Can Aris earn more than a conventional driver by selling hosted experiences? Probably yes, on paper.** The gain comes from earning more per day, not from doing more days.

| Per working day | Price | Aris's costs | Aris keeps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today: full-day driver (given costs) | Rp 900,000 | ~Rp 600,000 | **~Rp 300,000** |
| Bali Through Local Eyes (car) | Rp 1,600,000 | ~Rp 900,000 | **~Rp 700,000** |
| Kintamani Coffee & Volcano (car) | Rp 1,500,000 | ~Rp 920,000 | **~Rp 580,000** |
| Solo Companion, full day (scooter)* | Rp 1,100,000 | ~Rp 360,000 | **~Rp 740,000** |

\*Scooter figures depend on the legal and insurance checks in section 11. Until those are cleared, the safe version of every experience uses a car.

At roughly **3–4 experience bookings a week**, Aris clears Rp 5,000,000/month after costs. As a driver he needs about 17 full days a month for the same money.

**Not yet proven:**

- That strangers will pay these prices without many reviews. There is one review so far, and it was written by the person building the site.
- That Aris can legally sell guided, transported experiences as he works today (section 11).
- That he enjoys hosting, not just driving. The concept only works if this is genuinely him.

**The simplest credible proof:** run **10 paid pilot experiences in 6–8 weeks**, in a car, at the proposed prices, sold mostly to people who don't know Aris. Measure four things:

1. **Enquiry-to-booking rate.** Target: 25% or more.
2. **What he actually keeps per booking.** Target: Rp 500,000 or more on average.
3. **Review quality.** Target: at least 8 of 10 guests leave a 5-star review unprompted by friendship.
4. **Repeat or referral enquiries.** Target: at least 2.

If the pilot passes, launch. If it fails on price, keep driving and add one experience as a premium option. If it fails on demand, stop and stay a driver.

## 3. The three flagship experiences

All three are private for 1–4 guests, planned with the guest on WhatsApp before the day, and include pickup in South Bali or Ubud.

### A · Bali Through Local Eyes

*Full day, about 8–9 hours, by car.*

**What it is:** a day of ordinary Balinese life with someone who lives it. Aris builds the day around the guest's interests from a set of stops he actually knows.

**Possible stops**, in the order of a typical day (Aris confirms which he can genuinely offer):
1. An early morning traditional market, with breakfast snacks there.
2. Breakfast or coffee at a family-run warung.
3. A traditional village, with an explanation of the compound layout, family temples and daily offerings.
4. A temple visit where appropriate, with sarongs provided.
5. One or two local artisans or small family businesses (carving, weaving, silver, offerings).
6. Lunch at a warung Aris uses himself.
7. Conversation throughout: ceremonies, the Balinese calendar, the banjar, everyday life.

**Included:** car with fuel, parking and tolls; market tastings and warung coffee; temple donations and sarong hire; water.
**Not included:** lunch, entry tickets at paid attractions, purchases.
**House rules:** no commission shopping stops (Aris already promises this), and no "Instagram swing" stops unless the guest asks.

**Proposed price:** **Rp 1,600,000 (≈ A$130) for 1–2 guests**, plus Rp 250,000 per extra guest, up to 4 people.

### B · Solo Traveller Companion

*Half day (about 5 hours) or full day (about 9 hours). By scooter as a passenger, or by car.*

**What it is:** a friendly, knowledgeable local who makes exploring easier and more comfortable for someone travelling alone. The guest chooses the shape of the day.

**Possible days:**
- First-day orientation: how things work, money, SIM cards, what to pay, what to wear, where to eat.
- A countryside and rice-field half day.
- A local food morning.
- A full day built around the guest's interests.

Jay's real review describes exactly this: Kintamani coffee, Tegallalang lunch and an Ubud drop-off.

**Positioning rules:**
- **Do say:** comfort, safety, personal attention, trust.
- **Don't say:** anything romantic or about nightlife, and no "your Bali buddy" language. Keep photos and copy about places and conversation.

**Trust and safety (important for solo women travellers in particular):**
- Pickup and drop-off at reception or the hotel entrance only, never inside rooms.
- The route is agreed in writing beforehand.
- Guests are encouraged to share their live location.
- A helmet that meets SNI standards, with a fresh liner.
- Aris keeps to a clear professional code. Section 11 covers safeguarding.

**Proposed prices:**
- Half day: **Rp 700,000 (≈ A$57)**
- Full day: **Rp 1,100,000 (≈ A$89)**
- By car instead: +Rp 550,000

### C · Kintamani Coffee & Volcano

*Full day from South Bali; half day possible only from Ubud.*

**What it is:** the most photographable and easiest product to explain. Coffee overlooking Mount Batur, a real coffee producer, the caldera views, lunch with the volcano in front of you.

**Typical day:**
1. A scenic route north through villages and rice fields.
2. A coffee-producer visit and tasting, with an explanation of how Kintamani arabica is grown and processed.
3. Coffee at a viewpoint over Mount Batur and the lake.
4. Lunch overlooking the caldera.
5. One optional extra stop chosen by the guest (Tegallalang, a temple, a hot spring).

**Challenge:**
- **"Half day" isn't realistic from Seminyak or Canggu.** It's roughly 2 hours each way in traffic (assumption; Aris to confirm). Sell it as a full day, with a half-day version from Ubud.
- **Coffee "plantation" visits are often tourist shops.** Use a real producer Aris knows. Disclose any commission, or better, take none and say so.
- **Civet coffee (kopi luwak):** don't build the product around it. Caged civet production has welfare problems, and many guests now avoid it.

**Included:** car with fuel, parking and tolls; coffee tasting and a coffee at the viewpoint; any Kintamani entry fees (amount to be verified).
**Not included:** lunch, optional extra stops with their own entry fees.

**Proposed price:**
- **Car, 1–2 guests:** Rp 1,500,000 (≈ A$121), plus Rp 250,000 per extra guest.
- **Solo by scooter:** Rp 1,100,000 (the same as the Solo Companion full day).

### 5d. Bookings that lose money or barely pay

Not every booking is profitable:
- **A short airport run with a car hired just for it:** Rp 300,000 price against Rp 350,000+ car hire, so it **loses ~Rp 50,000–100,000**. Only do these on days the car is already hired.
- **Kintamani as a "half day" from South Bali in a full-day hire car:** if sold at Rp 900,000, costs are ~Rp 820,000, so it **barely breaks even**. Don't offer it.
- **Any experience sold through a platform at 30% commission with 3–4 guests' tastings included:** a Rp 1,500,000 booking for 4 guests keeps only ~Rp 330,000. Cap included tastings per booking, not per person.
- **Rain cancellations on the day, after the car is hired:** the full car cost is lost unless the rental place allows changes. Take a deposit (section 9).

### 5e. Scenarios: 5, 10 and 15 bookings per week

**Booking mix assumed:**
- 30% Local Eyes
- 35% Kintamani
- 20% Solo half day
- 15% Solo full day

That gives an average price of **Rp 1,310,000**. After variable costs and fees, the average booking leaves **~Rp 500,000**. On average each booking takes 0.9 of a working day.

**Capacity assumption:** Aris can host about 5.5 days a week sustainably. Bookings beyond that need a partner host or driver, at about Rp 450,000 per day (assumption). That person would also need the right licences (section 11).

| | **5 / week** | **10 / week** | **15 / week** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days of hosting needed per week | 4.5 | 9 | 13.5 |
| Partner days needed per week | 0 | 3.5 | 8 |
| **Gross revenue / month** | Rp 28.4M (≈ A$2,300) | Rp 56.7M (≈ A$4,590) | Rp 85.1M (≈ A$6,890) |
| Net contribution / month (after variable costs, fees, partner pay) | Rp 10.8M | Rp 14.8M | Rp 16.9M |
| **Aris's earnings / month** (after fixed costs, before tax) | **Rp 9.0M (≈ A$730)** | **Rp 13.0M (≈ A$1,050)** | **Rp 15.0M (≈ A$1,220)** |
| Gross revenue per year | Rp 341M | Rp 681M | Rp 1.02B |

**For comparison, as a driver today:** 5 full days a week at Rp 900,000 brings in Rp 19.5M gross a month, and Aris keeps **~Rp 5.8M**.

**What the scenarios tell us:**
1. **About 5 bookings a week is the sweet spot for one person.** It earns roughly 1.5× his driver income for the same days.
2. **Beyond about 6 a week, growth mostly enriches partners.** Going from 10 to 15 bookings adds only ~Rp 2M a month for Aris, plus a lot of coordination and risk. Grow through **price and group size** (extra-guest fees), not volume.
3. **Aris needs only about 3–4 bookings a week to beat his Rp 5M target.**
4. **Sensitivity:** if 60% of bookings came through platforms instead of 30%, the average booking would keep ~Rp 400,000 instead of ~Rp 500,000. Direct bookings matter.
5. **Tax:** under Indonesia's small-business rules (PP 55/2022), individual businesses under Rp 500M turnover a year are understood to be exempt from the 0.5% final tax. That is my reading and needs checking with a tax adviser. The 10/week scenario would cross that line.

These scenarios assume steady weeks. Real years have high season (July–August, Christmas–New Year), quiet months and ceremony days. A first year averaging **3–5 bookings a week** would be a good result.

## 9. Booking and operational workflow

1. **Enquiry:** WhatsApp, or the site's form, which opens WhatsApp. Aris replies within a few hours using saved quick replies.
2. **Plan and quote:** confirm the date, number of guests, pickup area and interests. Send a short written plan and the total price, with inclusions and exclusions in one message.
3. **Deposit:** 30% by payment link, enough to cover the car hire. The booking is confirmed only when the deposit arrives. Log it in the Sheet and Calendar.
4. **Confirmation message:** the itinerary, pickup time and place, what to wear (temples need covered shoulders and knees; sarongs provided), the cancellation policy and safety notes.
5. **Day before:** a reminder, a weather check and an offer to swap the plan if rain is likely.
6. **The day:** start with a safety briefing, especially for scooter days. Keep to the agreed plan unless the guest changes it, and confirm any change in price before it happens.
7. **Payment of the balance:** cash in IDR or the payment link, at the end of the day.
8. **Same evening:** a thank-you WhatsApp with the tourbaliparadise.com/review link (template already written).
9. **Weekly:** update the Sheet with bookings, price, costs, what Aris kept, the source of the booking and review status. This Sheet is the evidence for the pilot.

**Cancellation policy (proposal):**
- More than 48 hours before: full refund.
- 24–48 hours before: 50% kept.
- Less than 24 hours before: deposit kept.
- If Aris cancels, or for unsafe weather or ceremony closures: free reschedule or full refund.

Publish the policy on the site and repeat it in every confirmation message.

2. **Accommodation partners:** guesthouses, homestays and small villas in Ubud and Canggu whose guests match the profiles.
   - Offer a transparent referral fee. 10% is an assumption; the guest is told.
   - Give them a printed card and a WhatsApp link.
   - Start with 5 places Aris already knows.
3. **Instagram and TikTok.** Short, real clips from actual days (with guest consent): the market at 7am, coffee at the Batur viewpoint, Aris explaining an offering.
   - Post 2–3 times a week. No paid ads until the pilot shows conversion.
   - Use the language guests use: "solo in Bali", "first time in Bali", "Bali culture".
4. **Travel communities.** Solo-travel and Bali Facebook groups and Reddit.
   - Follow each group's rules on self-promotion. Usually that means answering questions helpfully and sharing only when asked.
   - No fake "I found this amazing guide" posts.
5. **Booking platforms (Phase 2–3).** List Kintamani only, priced so the 25–30% commission still leaves at least Rp 450,000. Use platforms for reach and early reviews, and steer repeat and referral guests direct.
6. **Organic content.** A few honest guides on the site, for example "What to wear to a Balinese temple", "Kintamani: what's worth it", "Your first day in Bali". Written with Aris's knowledge. They build search traffic over months.
7. **Airbnb Experiences.** Only once licences are confirmed. Airbnb's own Bali help pages say a tour guide needs a KTPP licence ([Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.co.in/help/article/2105)).

**Collecting reviews:** the system is already built:
- the /review link
- the QR card for the car
- the WhatsApp thank-you template
- reviews shown against the service each guest booked

Ask every guest, every time, the same evening. Never offer discounts for reviews, never write them, and never ask friends to post as guests.

**Marketing integrity:**
- Real photos and videos only.
- No invented customer numbers, qualifications, "as seen in" claims or cultural credentials.
- Disclose any commission from partners.

**A design that works if the answers are unfavourable:**
- **If Aris can't get a KTPP quickly:** sell the experiences through, or alongside, a licensed travel bureau or a licensed guide partner while he takes the competency test.
- **If his rented car isn't compliant:** book the vehicle from a licensed tourism transport operator (car plus licensed driver, assumed Rp 600,000–800,000/day), with Aris as the host. That cuts what he keeps on car experiences by roughly Rp 150,000–350,000 per day, but A and C still beat driving.
- **If scooter tours can't be lawfully insured:** drop the scooter as a paid product. Solo Companion becomes a car or walking experience, at a higher price. Jay's trip shows the format works; the vehicle is replaceable.

**Safety and compliance must not be traded for margin.** One accident with an uninsured paying passenger would end the business.

## 12. 30-, 60- and 90-day roadmap

### Days 1–30: Validate (no new marketing spend)

**Aris interview (most important).** Confirm in his own words:
- where he's from and his family's village
- which markets, warungs, artisans and coffee producers he genuinely knows
- languages
- whether he enjoys explaining culture
- when he's available
- current earnings and costs

Drop or reshape anything he can't honestly deliver.

**Compliance:**
- Book one meeting with a Bali business and legal adviser to answer checklist rows 1–4 and 6.
- Ask HPI Bali about the KTPP process, timing and cost.

**Customer research:**
- Talk to 10 past guests or travellers.
- Talk to 5 accommodation hosts in Ubud and Canggu.
- Ask what they'd pay for A and C, and what would stop them booking.
- Record the answers in the Sheet.

**Dry runs:**
- Run each flagship once with a friendly guest, at cost, to time the routes, check real costs (entry fees, Kintamani travel time) and take real photos.
- Set up the Google Business Profile. Collect the first genuine reviews.

### Days 31–60: Pilot launch

- **Site update:** on this branch, put the three experiences at the top of the homepage and give each its own page. Keep transfers and driver hire as practical services. Use real photos from the dry runs.
- **Workflow:** set up the payment link, the deposit and the cancellation policy, plus WhatsApp quick replies and labels.
- **Pilot target:** 10 paid experiences from people who don't know Aris.
- **Partners:** sign up 3–5 accommodation partners with referral cards.
- **Reviews:** keep collecting them: the QR card and a same-evening message, every time.

### Days 61–90: Decide, then expand carefully

- **Score the pilot:**
  - enquiry-to-booking rate
  - what Aris keeps per booking
  - reviews
  - repeat and referral enquiries
  - what guests said they'd change
- **Change prices only on the evidence:**
  - If most enquiries book instantly, raise prices by Rp 100,000–200,000.
  - If many enquiries never book, find out why before cutting prices.
- **Add one extra:** the Cultural Orientation, or the Food Trail if guests ask for it.
- **List on a platform:** consider listing Kintamani on one booking platform.
- **Only after positive margins hold for 8+ weeks:** consider a partner host for overflow, shared Kintamani departures in high season, and a concierge package.

**Don't expand until the three core experiences show real demand and positive margins.**
