Your first day in Bali

A few things to know when you land at Ngurah Rai, so your first day is easy and the rest of the trip goes smoothly.

Plan your first days

Getting from the airport

Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) is in South Bali, close to Kuta, Jimbaran and Seminyak and further from Ubud. A pre-arranged private transfer means no negotiating after a long flight. See airport transfer prices by area.

Entry rules and the tourist levy

You’ll usually need a visa, the free All Indonesia arrival card and the Bali tourist levy. See Bali entry requirements and official links for what each one is, what it costs and where to get it safely.

Money

The currency is the Indonesian rupiah (IDR). Prices have lots of zeros: Rp 100,000 is a common note. Cards work in many hotels and cafés, but carry some cash for warungs, markets and small shops. Use ATMs attached to banks where you can.

SIM cards and internet

Tourist SIM cards and eSIMs are easy to get on arrival, at the airport or in phone shops. You’ll need your passport to register a local SIM. Wi-Fi is common in hotels and cafés.

Getting around

Traffic in South Bali is heavy, so plan fewer stops per day than the map suggests. Ride-hailing apps like Grab and Gojek are handy for short trips. For full days out, a private driver is usually easier.

Customs worth knowing

  • Cover shoulders and knees, and wear a sarong and sash, when visiting temples. See Bali temple etiquette.
  • Try not to step on the small daily offerings you’ll see on pavements and doorsteps.
  • Use your right hand to give or receive things.
  • Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, closes the whole island, including the airport, for a day each year, usually in March. Check the date if you’re travelling around then.

Start your trip with a local

Many visitors like to spend an early day with someone who can explain Bali, from offerings and temples to what things should cost. Have a look at Experience Bali as a Local or, if you’re travelling alone, the Solo Traveller Companion.

Questions

How do I get from Bali airport to my hotel?

Pre-arrange a private transfer, use the official airport taxi counter, or use a ride-hailing app. A private transfer has a fixed price agreed in advance.

Do I need cash in Bali?

Yes, some. Many places take cards, but warungs, markets and small shops often prefer cash.

What happens on Nyepi?

Nyepi is the Balinese Day of Silence. The island shuts down for a day, including the airport, and visitors are asked to stay inside their accommodation.
